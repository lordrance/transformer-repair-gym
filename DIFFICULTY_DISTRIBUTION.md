# DIFFICULTY_DISTRIBUTION

Difficulty is keyed on the **independent FULL_FIX label**, never on reward.
Guardrails G1 and G3: a pass rate on an unaudited verifier is not a capability
measurement, and reward is a proxy for intent, not intent.

All numbers generated by `scripts/analyze_results.py` from
`artifacts/tier_m_audit.json` etc. Raw trajectories in `artifacts/`.

The target is a **gradient across the set**, not a high average. A task whose four
rollouts all earn the same reward contributes exactly zero GRPO advantage —
asserted as an identity in
`tests/test_minimal_grpo.py::test_constant_reward_group_produces_zero_advantage`
— so uniformly-easy and uniformly-hard sets are equally worthless.

### Both Tier M failures are the *same* failure mode

Not noise. Both PARTIAL_FIX rollouts fixed **one** of the two defects and stopped:

| rollout | expected files | edited | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `m4` e2 | `optim.py`, `train.py` | **`optim.py` only** | weight decay fixed, scheduler over-stepping left |
| `m5` e0 | `data.py`, `model.py` | **`data.py` only** | labels fixed, loss denominator left |

Localization rate is 18/20; both misses are *incomplete* localization on the two
multi-file tasks. That is the signal that told us what Tier H had to change: the
lever is not obscurity, it is **whether fixing one defect is enough**.

## Tier H — interacting defects, redesign round 1

Built after the Tier M measurement, per §9. Five tasks, 2–3 interacting defects
each, same families (see `TASK_CHAINS.md`). Manifest `tier_h_v2`.

Discrimination verified before any model saw them (`scripts/audit_repo_tasks_h.py`):
gold passes every check on all 5, the buggy repo fails only hidden checks on all 5,
and the visible suite passes on all 5 buggy repos — so the naive/hardened exploit
gap survives at H.

`h5_masking_triple_defect` is **held out**: graded once, at the end, with no task
or verifier adjustment afterwards.

### What made H harder: interaction, not obscurity

Every H task reuses its parent's mathematics. The change is that fixing one defect
is no longer sufficient. Evidence that the added difficulty is being paid in
*search*:

| | Tier M | Tier H |
|---|---|---|
| localization rate | 0.90 | **0.45** |
| FULL_FIX | 90 % | **40 %** |
| mean reads / distinct files | 10.4 / 9.4 | 10.5 / 9.4 |
| prompt tokens per turn | 6,888 | 7,100 (+3 %) |

Investigation *effort* is unchanged; investigation *success* halves. The Tier M
failure analysis predicted this precisely — both M failures were incomplete
localization on the two multi-file tasks.

### Cross-model check (`deepseek-v4-pro`, Tier M, 2 episodes/task)

| task | pro | flash |
|---|---|---|
| m1 / m2 / m3 / m4 | 2/2 each | 4/4 each |
| **m5** | **0/2** | 3/4 |

The ordering is consistent — `m5` is hardest for both, and both fail it the same
way (edit `data.py`, miss `model.py`). The *level* is not monotonic in model
strength: pro is worse on `m5`. With n = 2 that is an observation, not a result.


## Redesign budget

| round | trigger | status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3/5 Tier M tasks at 4/4 | **used** — Tier H, produced a spread |
| 2 | — | **unused, and will stay unused** |

Tier M results are frozen and retained in full; Tier H does not replace them. If
the set is still uniformly easy or uniformly hard after round 1, the outcome is
recorded as `DIFFICULTY_CALIBRATION_FAILED` and no third round is taken. Tuning
until a pass rate looks healthy would be fitting the benchmark to one model, which
is the specific failure this budget exists to prevent.

## What the difficulty numbers do *not* mean

- **Not a capability ranking.** `deepseek-v4-flash` is a strong model with tools
  and 14 turns against a 500-line codebase. That says nothing about a 4B policy.
- **Not transferable to an RL policy.** Guardrail G3: a fixed verifier degrades as
  the policy strengthens. These rates are for a frozen model with no optimization
  pressure.
- **Not precise.** n = 4 per task. A single rollout moves a task between classes.
  No significance is claimed anywhere in this project.
